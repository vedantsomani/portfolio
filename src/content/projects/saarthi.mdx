<CaseSection title="Objective">
  A flight controller for fixed-wing and glider airframes that keeps actuator authority away from
  the flight computer. The STM32H753 runs the flight stack; a separate STM32G431 owns RC input, the
  safety switch, and every PWM output, so the flight computer never drives a servo pin directly.
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="Constraints">
  The board is a chamfered 90 × 90 mm square with an 80 × 80 mm M3 pattern and a 30.5 × 30.5 mm
  stack pattern. It is a flight controller, not a power board: battery, motor, and servo current
  stay on a separate 3S/4S power distribution board, which feeds the controller regulated 5 V. Each
  IMU sits on its own SPI bus.
</CaseSection>

    Three IMUs on separate SPI buses and two barometers from different vendors give the firmware
    independent sources to cross-check. The firmware side is Saarthi FlightCore: a portable C11 core
    built and tested on a host PC, with Mahony, Madgwick-style, complementary, and reduced-MEKF
    attitude estimators behind one interface. It has no STM32 backend yet.
  </p>

<CaseSection title="Failures and iterations">
  <p>
    Two drafts came first: a 50 × 50 mm glider controller (40 mm failed DRC) and an STM32F745 board
    at 80 × 60 mm. The H7-Pro grew to 90 mm to carry its connectors. Its first ERC run reported 126
    errors; annotation brought that to 20, and later passes to 0.
  </p>
  <p>
    A September 2026 audit found BMI088 ground pins tied to the sensor supply, and the 3.3 V sensor
    regulator fed from 3.3 V, leaving it no headroom. Both are fixed in the current file (the
    regulator now takes 5 V). A two-board, six-layer revision is planned but not started.
  </p>
</CaseSection>
